You are given observations of human choices in mixed-gamble problems.

Each problem presents two options:
- Option A: a risky gamble that may involve gains and/or losses
- Option B: an alternative option, often a certain outcome

You will also see:
- a sequence of training trials from a single participant.

Important:
- Each decision is independent.
- Feedback is not available.
- History is not informative and should not strongly influence behavior.
- Choices should primarily depend on the current gamble structure.

Your task is NOT to write code.

Instead, infer a concise but behaviorally predictive text-based personality/profile for this participant.

The goal is to summarize the participant’s underlying decision-making tendencies so that another language model can later imitate this participant on unseen mixed-gamble problems.

The profile should capture:
- risk preference,
- loss aversion,
- gain sensitivity,
- sensitivity to expected value,
- sensitivity to probability,
- willingness to accept risky gambles,
- avoidance of losses,
- consistency vs stochasticity,
- decisiveness/confidence,
- and any other important stable behavioral tendencies.

Important:
- Focus on predictive behavioral tendencies rather than surface-level descriptions.
- Infer the behavioral strategy behind the observed choices.
- The profile should help another model predict the probability that the participant chooses Option B on future trials.
- Do not describe every trial individually.
- Do not summarize the dataset mechanically.
- Avoid vague personality descriptions unrelated to decision behavior.

Behavioral guidance:
- If the participant strongly avoids losses, describe the strength of loss aversion.
- If the participant frequently accepts risky gambles, describe risk-seeking tendencies.
- If the participant behaves consistently, mention strong or deterministic tendencies.
- If the participant behaves inconsistently or noisily, mention stochasticity or unstable preferences.
- If the participant appears highly sensitive to expected value or probability, describe the strength of these effects.

You may include concise quantitative summaries when helpful, such as:
- approximate gamble acceptance rates,
- confidence levels,
- sensitivity strengths,
- consistency levels,
- or simplified behavioral rules.

Example:
- "The participant strongly avoids gambles involving large potential losses, even when the expected value is positive."
- "The participant is moderately risk-seeking and often accepts high-variance gambles."
- "The participant behaves consistently and follows a stable gain-versus-loss tradeoff rule."
- "The participant tends to reject gambles unless the potential gain substantially outweighs the possible loss."

Requirements:
- Output only the participant profile text.
- Do not output code.
- Do not output JSON.
- Do not output markdown headers.
- Keep the profile concise but sufficiently detailed to support downstream prediction.
