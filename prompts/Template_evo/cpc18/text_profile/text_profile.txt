You are given observations of human choices in the CPC18 Track II task (individual behavior).

Each trial presents two options (Option A and Option B) with lottery parameters such as:
- high outcomes,
- probabilities,
- low outcomes,
- ambiguity,
- correlation,
- lottery shape,
- and number of outcomes.

You will also see:
- a sequence of training trials from a single participant,
- including the participant’s previous choices and feedback history when available.

Your task is NOT to write code.

Instead, infer a concise but behaviorally predictive text-based personality/profile for this participant.

The goal is to summarize the participant’s underlying decision-making tendencies so that another language model can later imitate this participant on unseen CPC18 problems.

The profile should capture:
- risk preference,
- expected-value sensitivity,
- ambiguity sensitivity,
- correlation sensitivity,
- sensitivity to lottery structure or skewness,
- inertia or repetition tendencies,
- feedback sensitivity,
- adaptation across repeated trials,
- switching behavior after gains/losses,
- exploration vs exploitation tendencies,
- consistency vs stochasticity,
- decisiveness/confidence,
- and any other important behavioral patterns or strategy changes over time.

Important:
- Focus on predictive behavioral tendencies rather than surface-level descriptions.
- Infer the behavioral strategy behind the observed choices.
- The profile should help another model predict the probability that the participant chooses Option B on future trials.
- Do not describe every trial individually.
- Do not summarize the dataset mechanically.
- Avoid vague personality descriptions unrelated to decision behavior.

Behavioral guidance:
- If the participant behaves consistently, mention strong or deterministic tendencies.
- If the participant behaves inconsistently or noisily, mention stochasticity or unstable preferences.
- If history strongly influences choices, describe how.
- If the participant adapts behavior after feedback or recent outcomes, describe the adaptation pattern.
- If the participant appears highly sensitive to expected value, ambiguity, correlation, or skewness, describe the strength of these effects.

You may include concise quantitative summaries when helpful, such as:
- approximate choice frequencies,
- confidence levels,
- sensitivity strengths,
- consistency levels,
- switching tendencies,
- or simplified behavioral rules.

Example:
- "The participant strongly prefers Option B when its expected value exceeds Option A, choosing B roughly 80–90% of the time in such cases."
- "Recent feedback strongly affects subsequent choices."
- "The participant becomes more risk-seeking after positive outcomes."
- "The participant behaves conservatively under ambiguity and tends to avoid highly skewed lotteries."

Requirements:
- Output only the participant profile text.
- Do not output code.
- Do not output JSON.
- Do not output markdown headers.
- Keep the profile concise but sufficiently detailed to support downstream prediction.
